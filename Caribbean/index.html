Caribbean Public Innovation Program

Caribbean residents learning to understand their own communities through insightful data and building solutions that fit island realities, in responsible ways.

In development in Saint Lucia with the Sir Arthur Lewis Community College

Summary

Local knowledge, modern tools, island-made solutions.

The Public Innovation Institute (PII) is developing a Caribbean innovation program that trains young people to understand their own communities through data, then use modern tools and technologies to build solutions that fit island realities, in responsible ways.

The program runs for eight weeks during the summer months.

The program is being developed in Saint Lucia with the Sir Arthur Lewis Community College (SALCC), and designed to extend to other islands across the region.

Why the Caribbean, and why now

Technology built elsewhere rarely fits here.

Most of the technology now reaching Caribbean governments and communities was designed elsewhere, and for populations, infrastructure and data systems that look very different from the Caribbean's. A disease surveillance tool built for a large mainland country, or a climate model trained on continental weather, often doesn't translate to a small island with a single hospital, a tourism-dependent economy and a coastline that is the whole country.

The region already lives with these pressures: narrow economies, high import dependence, limited fiscal space and some of the highest exposure to climate and disaster risk in the world. AI is arriving quickly, and it can either deepen dependence on imported solutions or give local people the tools to design their own. This program is built for the second path.

Approach

See the problem clearly first. Then build.

The program leans into two approaches. Participants first learn to see a problem clearly through data, and only then learn to build with AI and other novel tools.

Step 1 · Understand

Understanding hyperlocal challenges through data

Participants work with real data from their own island or community: public datasets, government records, field observations and conversations with the people affected. The goal is an in-depth, ground-level understanding of a challenge, down to the parish, district or neighbourhood, rather than a national average that hides what is really happening.

Participants are not handed a research question. They find the problem they believe matters most and make the case for it with evidence.

Step 2 · Build responsibly

Responsible innovation with AI and other technologies

With that understanding in hand, participants learn to use AI tools, data analysis and other technologies to design novel solutions. Responsibility is built in from day one: ethics of AI, privacy and consent, bias in data, and the question of who benefits and who carries the risk.

PII's Director for AI and Technology Ethics, Dr. Simona Tiribelli, leads this part of the curriculum.

Tracks

Five tracks. One region's biggest questions.

Participants choose a track that shapes the data they work with and the mentors they meet. The tracks below are a starting set; each island can weight them toward its own priorities.

Track 01

Biosecurity and population health

How would an island detect and respond to an outbreak early with limited lab and hospital capacity? Where are the gaps in health data, and how can AI help close them responsibly?

Track 02

Climate and coastal resilience

Which communities face the greatest risk from sea-level rise, storms and flooding? How can data guide adaptation and disaster recovery?

Track 03

Education

Where are students falling behind, and why? How can AI support teachers and learners without widening gaps?

Track 04

Infrastructure and public services

Which roads, water systems and public assets need attention first, and how can repairs be prioritized fairly?

Track 05

Food systems and economic resilience

How can islands reduce import dependence and strengthen local food production and livelihoods?

Policy and practice, connected. The biosecurity and population health track connects to PII's wider work on AI-enabled biological risk, including planned policy simulations with officials from Caribbean small island states. Findings from those simulations with government officials will feed directly into what participants learn.

How the program runs

From first dataset to final showcase.

The structure follows a model PII has already tested with cohorts in Cambridge and online, adapted for the Caribbean.

  1. 1
    Opening two weeks

    Foundations

    An intensive opening period covering data analysis, research methods, microeconomic theory, the ethics of AI and the human side of data, meaning the people and lives behind every dataset.

  2. 2
    Find the problem

    Discovery

    Participants explore data from their own country, alongside conversations with community members and officials, and choose the challenge they want to work on.

  3. 3
    Design & prototype

    Build

    Small teams use AI tools and other technologies to design and prototype a solution, with regular feedback from mentors in their track.

  4. 4
    Present & publish

    Showcase

    Teams present their work to government officials and partners. Strong projects are published on PII's platform and can be developed further with local institutions.

Live online

Teaching and mentorship happen live online through PII's learning platform, so participants can join from anywhere on the island or across the region.

In person

In-person sessions anchor the opening weeks and the final showcase, when being in the same room matters most.

Your work is yours

Participants keep ownership of the work they create. PII's past programs have drawn participants from more than 15 countries, many of whom have gone on to leading graduate programs and jobs in the public and private sectors.

Growing from Saint Lucia to the region

A design that travels from island to island.

Working with SALCC gives the program a home institution, local faculty and a group of students ready to start, and allows PII to shape the curriculum around Saint Lucian data and challenges.

The design is meant to travel. The curriculum, tracks and online platform stay shared, while each new island brings its own data, its own challenges and, ideally, its own partner institution. Participants on different islands working on similar problems, such as coastal flooding or outbreak preparedness, can learn from one another's approaches.

About PII

The Public Innovation Institute

The Public Innovation Institute is a Cambridge-based nonprofit working where technology, public policy and urban systems meet. PII's education programs, including Data, Social Innovation, and Public Policy, have reached young people from more than 15 countries across Africa, Europe, Latin America, the Caribbean and North America.

Most recently, PII's summer innovation program with an AI-enabled infrastructure data company had students build tools to help The City of Boston decide which roads and curb ramps to repair first, weighing safety, accessibility and neighbourhood need.

City-as-Innovation-Lab

The institute's City-as-Innovation-Lab initiative brings together local governments, industry, and academic partners to develop innovative solutions to pressing public challenges.

Hyperion

Our Hyperion program supports entrepreneurs developing solutions for public impact.

Research

PII conducts research on AI in cities with the Data-Smart City Solutions Program at Harvard University.
